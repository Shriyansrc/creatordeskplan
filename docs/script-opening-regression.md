## What changes in the writer

- A stranger can recognise the situation from the opening itself.
- Tension and context arrive together, not a slow introduction before the hook.
- The reason to stay is a small concrete payoff the body can actually deliver.
- The first usable line, action or distinction appears early.
- Optional context adds a cause, constraint or contrast. Repeated fear is cut.
- Hook/body alternatives remain compatible with the same promised payoff.
- Topic-led length and format remain. No mandatory equal paragraphs, stock story or forced pain opening.
- One goal-matched CTA; no invented free resource or DM promise.

## Authored review cases, not generation evidence

These illustrate the acceptance rubric only. They are NOT embedded in the production prompt and must not become defaults for every topic.

### New-group conversation, Hinglish

Only under an explicit new-group topic, a plausible opening is:

"Naye group mein sab baat kar rahe hain, aur tum soch rahe ho: beech mein bolun kya? Perfect introduction mat dhoondo. Jo baat chal rahi hai, usi se ek sawaal uthao."

The next beat must give usable wording, for example if people are discussing a class:

"Tum logon ko aaj ki class kaunsa part mushkil laga?"

Then explain why it connects to the conversation instead of demanding a new topic. Do not claim this guarantees friends or confidence. Do not restate that the viewer is afraid of saying something wrong.

### Domain and hosting, English

"Your website quote lists a domain and hosting as two separate charges. Before you pay, here's what each one actually buys."

The body must immediately distinguish address/name from the service that serves site files, then give a concrete buying check. Do not copy a saved creator's restaurant analogy or invent prices.

### Single posting check, brief

"A stranger sees your first line without the title. Can they tell what problem your post solves?"

Deliver a short before/after or one actionable check. This idea does not need a long story, numbered framework or four balanced sections.
